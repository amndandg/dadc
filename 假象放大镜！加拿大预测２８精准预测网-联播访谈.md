TORONTO – The CFL’s deadliest passing combo and a veteran quarterback coming off a career year are the finalists for the league’s outstanding player award.

Montreal quarterback Davis Alexander and Alouettes receiver Tyson Philpot, along with Edmonton Elks quarterback Cody Fajardo, were announced Wednesday as the finalists for the CFL’s George Reed Most Outstanding Player award.

Alexander threw for 5,257 yards and 35 TDs this season, joining Alouettes offensive co-ordinator Anthony Calvillo as the only two Montreal players to crack the 5,000-yard plateau. Alexander also sported a 72.1 completion percentage and had a CFL-high 37 completions of 30-plus yards.

Philpot, of Delta, B.C., led the CFL in catches (124), yards (1,809), 30-plus yard receptions (14) and yards after the catch (750) and was tied with teammate Tyler Snead for most TD grabs (both 13). Philpot also set league marks for most catches and yards in a season by a Canadian player and is a finalist for the league’s top Canadian award.

Fajardo set career highs in passing yards (4,788) and TDs (25) in leading Edmonton (13-5) atop the West Division for the first time since 2015. The 34-year-old quarterback also ran for 517 yards (5.7-yard average) and eight touchdowns.

Linebackers Adarius Pickett of Toronto and Darnell Sankey of B.C., along with Calgary defensive lineman Clarence Hicks, were named finalists for the CFL’s top defensive player award. Winnipeg’s Trey Vaval is chasing a second straight outstanding special-teams honour — attempting to become the league’s first back-to-back winner — with Calgary returner Tyreik McAllister and Edmonton kicker Vincent Blanchard also in the running.

Pickett registered a CFL-leading 116 total tackles and was second overall with 103 defensive tackles (six for loss). He also finished second in defensive plays (134) while adding 13 special-teams tackles, three sacks and two forced fumbles.

Sankey had a league-high defensive plays (136) and defensive tackles (114, including a career-high four for a loss). Sankey also recorded three sacks to tie a career best.

Hicks led the CFL in sacks with 12, which also matched his career best. Hicks appeared in all 18 of Calgary’s regular-season games, registering 24 tackles (four for a loss), three pass knockdowns and two forced fumbles.

Vaval led the CFL in total kick-return yards (2,868) in his sophomore season while averaging 13.8 yards per punt return and 24.2 yards on kickoffs. Vaval scored two return TDs — an 88-yard kickoff return and a 120-yard missed field goal return.

McAllister scored three return TDs this season (two missed field goals, one punt). He also had a career-best 2,691 return yards.

Blanchard was the CFL’s most accurate kicker (making 55-of-59 field goals, 93.2 per cent). He registered a league-record nine field goals in Edmonton’s 37-35 overtime win over Hamilton on Sept. 26 and led the CFL in scoring with 211 points.

The league won’t be honouring its top players at an annual awards ceremony this year. Instead, the winners will learn of their accomplishment through surprise presentations that will be shared online.

Cheryl Ramage is proud to have two sons in the Canadian Armed Forces. Both were training at CFB Wainwright when a fire ripped through the base earlier this month.

The mother of four was attending dinner theatre when friends sent a text message asking: “Are your boys OK?”

“I had no idea what was happening,” she recalled.

Cheryl immediately texted her sons. She heard back from her son Rowan, who was safe. But her eldest, Liam, did not respond.

She checked in with Liam’s wife, Marisa Ramage, who also hadn’t been able to connect with him.

The women met at Liam and Marisa’s home at the Edmonton Garrison.

They scoured the internet for information, and at 3 a.m., they read that the military was keeping communications to a minimum while they notified next of kin.

It took time for them to learn the full extent of the fast-moving fire on Oct. 17 that killed four soldiers and left 39 others injured during a training exercise at the base located southeast of Edmonton.

“Then the doorbell rang,” Cheryl said, dabbing her eyes with tissue. “That was the hardest moment of my life. They walked in and said they had devastating news.”

Marisa assumed the worst. She said she couldn’t stop shaking, like she was in shock.

“I thought Liam was … I thought he was dead,” she said.

Marisa said the military officers started to explain, but she couldn’t bear waiting.

“I did not let them finish their conversation. I went, ‘He’s not dead, right?’ And they were like, ‘No, he is not dead. But he’s got some major, major injuries.'”

With that, the women rushed to Edmonton’s Royal Alexandra Hospital. They weren’t prepared for what they saw.

“We had to identify him by his feet,” Liam’s mother explained.

“Seeing him in a bed with burns on him, I couldn’t be in the room,” his wife said.

Doctors told them 40 per cent of Pte. Ramage’s body had been burned.

“Mostly to his face, head, neck, his hands are bad, both the tops and his palms, both arms, both legs and some on his back as well,” Cheryl explained.

In a moment alone with her husband of three years, Marisa whispered in his ear.

“You are too stubborn to die and there is no way that you’re not making it through this.”

Since that night, the 27-year-old from Red Deer County has undergone multiple surgeries and skin grafts, but the full extent of his injuries is still unknown.

“He can’t see when he wakes up, he can’t speak and he can’t move very much, so he has really no way of communicating,” his mother explained.

Cheryl said her son’s life has already been saved by blood donors, and she encourages people wanting to help to roll up their sleeves and donate.

She says she was told despite his own severe injuries, Pte. Ramage was still trying to save his fellow soldiers when first responders arrived.

That doesn’t surprise his wife, who says he loves to help people.

Marisa doesn’t know what the couple’s future holds, but she’s grateful for every small step her husband is making.

“It’s nice to see him improve. He can move his arms on his right side and his foot on his right side. He can nod yes or no and he can move his mouth.”

His mom says when Pte. Ramage enlisted in the Canadian Armed Forces in 2024, it had been a long time coming.

“He’s always been passionate about the military. That’s really all he’s ever wanted to do,” Cheryl explained.

She said he got his first taste of the military in cadets, where he advanced to the top as a Regimental Sergeant Major.

Since enlisting in 2024, Pte. Ramage has served in Latvia and at the G7 in Kananaskis, Alta.

His mom never thought her son would be in danger on home soil, calling the fire “a freak accident.”

The disaster remains under investigation.

The family is putting together photo albums to bring to Pte. Ramage’s hospital room.

“His smile lit up the room. He’s infectious. He’s a storyteller. He talks nonstop. And that’s what all of his buddies say too. Liam just never stopped talking.”

When he’s not working, Pte. Ramage loves playing bagpipes, cooking and Canada’s game.

“His passion is hockey. Hockey, hockey, more hockey. A diehard Montreal Canadiens fan,” his mom laughed.

The family brought a radio to the hospital this week, so as Pte. Ramage heals, they can listen to games together.

“We’ve created a GoFundMe for Liam. It’s going to be a long road, months if not years of rehabilitation, surgeries,” Cheryl explained.

They’re grateful for the support of the military, including their designated assistant, and plan to continue to stay with Pte. Ramage every day in the hospital.

“He’s got an amazing support system,” Marisa said.

“His military friends have been also phenomenal. They’ve told us stories and they’ve been visiting him, which I think is really good for his mental state.”
